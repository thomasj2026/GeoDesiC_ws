# GeoDesiC Lab Research Work

## About This Repository

This repository is maintained by **Thomas Istvan** as a personal research workspace for tracking my research, code development, simulations, mathematical analysis, notes, and other work conducted under the supervision of **Dr. Jake Welde** in the **Geometry, Design, and Control (GeoDesiC) Laboratory** at **Cornell University**.

The GeoDesiC Lab is a robotics and control research group within Cornell University's **Sibley School of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering**. The lab investigates the role of **differential geometry in control synthesis and morphology design for robotic systems**.

## Research Focus

My work in the GeoDesiC Lab is conducted within the broader areas of **robotics, dynamics, and control**, with particular emphasis on the mathematical and computational tools used to understand, design, and control robotic systems.

The GeoDesiC Lab explores how concepts from **differential geometry** can be used in the synthesis of control algorithms and the design of robotic system morphology.

Research topics I am associated within the lab include but are not limited to:

- **Aerial Robotics and Manipulation**
  - Designing aerial robotic systems and manipulators capable of agile interaction and manipulation tasks.

- **Trajectory and Task-Space Planning**
  - Developing dynamically feasible trajectories for robotic systems, including underactuated aerial manipulators.

- **Learning-Based Control**
  - Investigating methods such as reinforcement learning and the use of system symmetry to improve sample efficiency and trajectory-tracking performance.

- **Autonomous Perception and Target Tracking**
  - Developing robotic systems capable of tracking moving objects using onboard sensing, perception, computation, and control.

My individual work documented in this repository may address a subset of these areas and will evolve as my research progresses.
The repository is intended to provide an organized record of the development and progression of my research within the GeoDesiC Lab.
